# Levers, adoption pitches, and product facts

Read at draft time, before any recommendation is written. Everything here is a starting point telling you what to check, never a citation: product state (SDK support, prices, names, settings locations, defaults) is never recorded in this skill, so the last column of each table is what to SEARCH FOR, never where to go (the two-kinds rule in `SKILL.md`). Where a fix names a config key, an SDK, a tier, or a version floor, that name is a search prompt and not a fact: confirm it on the page before it reaches a draft.

## Levers

Match the signal unit to the meter: `% of your bill` for products with their own line (replay, mobile replay, error tracking, AI Observability, surveys, pipelines, warehouse, feature flags); `% of your events` for what rolls into product analytics (autocapture, pageviews, custom events); a behavior count where frequency is the problem (identify, `$set`, `group()`). Do not translate events into "bill": a technical buyer notices. The `% of your events` rows need a per-event-name breakdown, which is readable in both regions through the direct ClickHouse connection (US via project 2, EU via the API path in `data-rules.md`): get the number or drop the parenthetical, and never infer one.

**Expansion** (setup is solid, so lead with the opportunity and no cost %): connect Stripe, a CRM, support, or a prod DB as a warehouse source, then join to events in SQL to answer revenue-and-behavior questions. Search: data warehouse sources; joining data. **There is no "revenue analytics" product, so never name one to a customer.** This rule gets challenged every few runs and the challenge is always wrong, because everything downstream of the parent page is still alive: `docs/revenue-analytics/start-here`, `capture-revenue-events`, `managed-views` and the rest all return 200, `docs-search` returns them confidently, and the `.mdx` files sit in `PostHog/posthog.com` with no deprecation frontmatter. **The removal notice lives only on the parent index, `posthog.com/docs/revenue-analytics`** ("The Revenue analytics dashboard has been removed"), so open that page and no other before touching this line. The capability survived; the dashboard and the name did not. Revenue is what they get alongside their product data once a revenue source is connected or revenue properties ride on their events: the properties land on persons and groups and are read from insights, SQL, managed views, and person and group profiles, so the customer builds the view their business needs rather than opening a dashboard. The mapping uses **their own event and property names**, so a purchase event already carrying `value` and `currency` needs no code change; read `revenue_tracking_config` on their team before prescribing anything: the column is on `postgres.posthog_team` and `eu_postgres_posthog_team` alike, it is **empty on almost every team**, and where it is populated it reads `{"events": [], "baseCurrency": "..."}`, so an empty `events` array means no revenue event is mapped even though the config exists. Either shape is why revenue shows nothing. Never name a settings page or nav path for it. Search: capturing revenue events. Which payment platforms are wired up changes, so read the page rather than promising a customer their processor is supported.

## Disqualifiers: right numbers that are not findings

Run this list against every candidate finding before it reaches a draft. Traps stop wrong numbers; this stops correct numbers being written up as problems. A disqualified item is dropped silently, not reported as a non-finding.

- **An unset setting is not a customer who ignored you.** A config read returns the fields you selected and nothing about the fields you did not, so "min duration, sample rate and triggers are all null" supports exactly that sentence and no more. It does not support "they never acted", "the advice was ignored", or "not one lever was applied". A customer told to cut replay cost can act through routes this query cannot see: switching the product off on a project, setting the billing limit to zero, removing the SDK from a surface, or dropping the events upstream. Check those before writing any sentence about what they did or did not do, because the blunt instruments are what a cost-pressed customer reaches for first, and "they ignored this" written over "they applied the heaviest version of this" is an insult rather than an opening.
- **A lever the customer has already pulled.** Check every candidate against `change-timeline` before it reaches a draft. Their own fix is invisible in the places a cost finding normally comes from: the last invoice is historical, the limits map shows the cap rather than the usage under it, and a 30-day total averages straight across the change. Prescribing it back to them is the one failure that proves nobody looked, and it is worse than sending nothing. What it converts to is a confirmation, worth one sentence with the date and the measured effect, then move to what is left.
- **Growth on a growing account.** Volume rising because the business is rising is the product working. It becomes a finding only when the unit cost moved, when it will cross a tier or free allowance, or when nobody on their side has seen the number.
- **A deliberate configuration.** Sampling, a paused sync, a retention tier, a billing limit someone chose. Never red on its own without evidence of harm. Check who set it and when before calling it a problem.
- **A product under its free tier.** Real volume billing $0.00 is not a cost finding, whatever the raw count looks like; the honest framing there is adoption, not spend. Read the invoice line before writing a cost sentence.
- **A high identified share on logged-in traffic.** Correct by definition on an app behind a login. It is only a finding on public marketing pages.
- **A plan default nobody chose.** A limits map identical across an org's siblings is a default, not a decision, and the finding is the absence of a real limit elsewhere rather than the presence of that one.
- **A product they never bought showing zero.** Only a paid product at zero is the "paid but unused" finding.
- **A timezone or locale that looks wrong.** A project timezone far from the legal entity's country is routinely correct, because the product serves that market. Confirm against where their users actually are before reporting it.
- **Mobile lifecycle events that look like custom instrumentation.** They carry no `$` and are SDK autocapture. Subtract them first, per the rule in `data-rules.md`.
- **A count that only looks small because the query truncated.** Rows equal to the LIMIT is truncation. Re-run the tail before it becomes a finding about absence.

## Product facts

Starting points, not citations. Each one names what to check and where the common misconception sits. Find the page with a live `docs-search`; docs are the primary citation, and a PostHog-authored `posthog.com/tutorials` page is acceptable only when no docs page covers the topic.

**Some of these have no doc home, and searching for them actively misleads.** The `before_send` allowlist trap, the credits policy, and the consolidation approach are PostHog's own knowledge rather than documented behavior: a search returns loosely related SDK pages beside Community Questions and GitHub issues, which this skill never cites. Confirm those against the customer's own config or data, or state them as our read and not as a doc-backed fact. Never promote a near-miss page into a citation to satisfy the verification rule: a fact with no link is honest, a fact with the wrong link is not.

- **Feature flags**: every `/flags` call is 1 billable request, and one request returns the whole flag set, so the bill counts requests and not flags. In the Python SDK `evaluate_flags()` (one call per user or run, read the snapshot) supersedes the deprecated `get_feature_flag()` / `get_all_flags()`; local evaluation asks for a feature flags secure API key rather than a personal key. Confirm both on the live page before quoting. **Check which SDK is making the calls before recommending anything, because the two levers do not overlap.** Local evaluation is server-side SDKs only, so on client-side flag traffic (read the per-SDK split) prescribing it is a wrong answer, not a weak one; bootstrapping is theirs, and which SDKs support it is product state, so read the page before telling anyone theirs cannot. Local evaluation polls cached definitions and each poll bills as 10 requests, so on a server fleet at the default interval it is not automatically cheaper at scale. Edge, lambda, and other stateless runtimes cannot hold the cache: give them an external cache or regular evaluation. Evaluate a flag once, record the result, and re-evaluate only on a meaningful change. **Deleting or deactivating a flag in the UI saves nothing on either meter, so never prescribe it as a cost fix**: client SDKs fire `$feature_flag_called` whenever code evaluates a key, including keys entirely absent from the response, and only removing the call from the code stops either meter. So a flag-cleanup recommendation is a code-hygiene recommendation and should be worded that way, and a deactivated flag still taking heavy traffic is positive evidence the dead check is still shipped. When a flags bill confuses a customer, give each candidate cause an explicit verdict instead of naming only the best fit: a server SDK calling per check without local evaluation, local-evaluation polling at 10x per instance, and `identify()` reloading flags in the web SDK. Date the spike against deploys before blaming any of them, and check the flag billing limit while there: hitting it makes `/flags` return empty flag sets silently.
- **Identified events cost more than anonymous ones**, because PostHog processes a person profile for each. Never put a multiple in a draft, because the tiers change. Say they are more expensive, link the pricing page so they see the full breakdown against their own tier, and spend the paragraph on the lever instead.
- **`before_send` allowlists silently kill PostHog internal events (JS SDK).** Always check this when a customer shares one. An allowlist by event name drops everything else, including `$snapshot` (killing ALL recordings), `$pageleave`, `$feature_flag_called`, `$web_vitals`, survey events, and `$exception`. Confirm `$snapshot` is in the allowlist before calling their filter sound. The tell: `recording_count_in_period` collapses right after the filter deploys while the replay billing line stays under its limit. Fix: keep the internal events, or flip to a denylist.
- **Group analytics** requires identified events and will not work with `$process_person_profile: false`.
- **Cohorts**: dynamic behavioral cohorts cannot target flags, experiments, or surveys; duplicate as a static cohort first. Person-property-only dynamic cohorts work.
- **Experiments** are flag-powered. Which stats engine an experiment runs on is product state: read the page before naming one. Anonymous-at-exposure plus identified-at-conversion splits identity and double-counts: resolve identity first.
- **PostHog AI** ships with a default spend cap and free monthly credits, so never recommend setting a limit as if one is missing; read the current cap and credit numbers live before quoting either. Its MCP tools need AI data processing enabled.
- **Any pricing figure**: the docs search index lags the live site and has served a stale `/pricing` copy while the live page differed. When a number matters, fetch the live page and quote that; when two live pages disagree, quote neither and say the pages disagree.
- **Billing limits drop data permanently** when hit, and the loss is not only events: a replay limit drops the recordings, which are gone rather than deferred. Price the limit against the uncapped bill before defending it: a cap that saves tens of dollars while blacking out the last week of a cycle is a bad trade, and sampling buys the same money back with coverage all month.
- **Replay Vision** meters in **credits**, not observations, and credits per observation depend on the scanner's model, so a scan count never prices a month. Every org has a monthly free credit tier and a default credit budget; confirm both live rather than quoting them from here. Three things to raise unprompted, because none is visible to the customer: a scanner enabled mid-cycle looks trivially cheap until you take its daily rate times a full cycle, the usage report carries only aggregate credits and never names which scanner spent them, and a hit replay or product-analytics billing limit drops recordings at ingestion so the scanner goes blind while its spend stands. Before pitching a cut, read what the scanner is actually finding rather than only what it costs: its observations are queryable, and `replay-vision` in `queries-products.md` has the three routes and which one answers what.
- **Unused-event removal**: "unused by insights" is not "unused". An event a live experiment depends on can look idle. Confirm no experiment references it before recommending removal. `experiment-list`'s `event` filter covers events behind actions, but it runs against the active project: from project 2 it queries PostHog's own experiments and answers "nothing references it" about the wrong org. For a customer's event, ask them to check the "experiments using this event" section on their own event definition page rather than asserting it. To find unqueried events, join their events against `query_log_archive` on the direct ClickHouse connection, which works on **EU**; on **US** that table is not readable, so hand the customer the join to run in their own project and say why. Run from project 2 it answers about the wrong org, the same way. Do not use `app_metrics2`, which reflects tracked interactions rather than usage.
- **Autocapture** covers clicks, inputs, and form submissions; pageviews, rageclicks, and dead clicks are separate events. It is useful paired with Actions and is billable noise without them. The Toolbar builds Actions visually.
- **LLM cost**: the aggregation rules, the two event types that break a total in opposite directions, and the cache-hit branch all live in `## LLM cost` in `queries-money.md`, which owns them because that is where the queries are. Two things to carry into a recommendation from here: their PostHog AI spend and their product's own LLM spend are different numbers from different sources, and to explain a jump you check which of model mix, volume, prompt size or cache-hit rate moved.
- **Warehouse billable rows** are `rows_synced` where `billable AND status='Completed'`, not the static table `row_count`. Many `BillingLimitReached` jobs means they are repeatedly hitting their cap. Cheapest fix first: kill a duplicate cross-project sync, move large tables to incremental, extend the frequency.
- **Credits**: onboarding is flexible when it makes sense, and a misconfiguration-driven bill is the clearest case. Size the credit to the share the misconfiguration caused, not the whole invoice by default; when a billing limit capped the bill, size against the billed amount. A first invoice that is mostly waste leans toward a full one-time goodwill credit. Make it one-time and tie it to them fixing the root cause. Apply via the billing admin. The draft still never promises it unless the user has confirmed.
- **Competing-tool consolidation**: lead with the category PostHog already bills them for (LaunchDarkly next to millions of flag requests). Frame it as one vendor and one dataset, and frame it self-driving when that lands with them (clean data in one place, better signals, better PRs), not as a feature bake-off. Say "looks like you also run X", never "you rely on X", since a CSP hit is presence and not proof of use. One tool, one sentence: the full list belongs only in the brief. PostHog in-app but GA on the marketing site is its own angle, and tells you who owns what internally.
